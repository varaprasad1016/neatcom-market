The short version

I measured your website before writing this.

Everything in this document comes from testing your live site — not from a template. No guesswork, no generic advice. Here is what I found, in plain English.

01

Your website takes 7.3 seconds to show anything

A visitor clicking you from Google stares at a blank screen for over seven seconds. Google's own guideline is under 2.5 seconds.

02

Your homepage currently says "0%"

Three headline statistics are broken. Right now the page reads "0% Fleet Efficiency Improvement" and "0% Reduction in Vehicle Theft" to every visitor and to Google.

03

Nothing has been published in over two years

Six articles total, the newest from October 2024 — and it tells customers about a compliance deadline that passed 18 months ago.

04

Your most valuable product line gets 340 words

Tachograph compliance applies to every HGV and coach in the country, never stops, and puts an operator's licence at risk. Its page runs to 340 words. A London-only product whose deadline expired in 2024 gets 2,490.

05

Google can't properly read your 29 products

None carry the product information Google needs to show prices and availability in search results. Several have their specifications baked into images as pixels, which search engines cannot read at all.

The good news

Your foundations are sound. Page titles are written properly, your site is secure, links resolve correctly, and nothing jumps around as the page loads. Your DVS Progressive Safe System page is genuinely strong — roughly 2,500 words on the most commercially urgent topic in UK fleet compliance. You don't need to start from nothing. You need the brakes taken off.

Your homepage is roughly 31 times heavier than a competitor doing the same job.

Why it's so heavy

Three causes, all fixable without redesigning anything:

  • 14 videos start playing the moment the page opens — about 4 MB before a single word is read. The same three clips load over and over.
  • Images were never compressed. One is a 782 KB screenshot of your app that is mostly flat black. It should be around 40 KB. Modern image formats are switched off entirely on your server.
  • The page loads 46 separate style files and 46 separate scripts. That's the page builder's default behaviour, left unconfigured.

What that means commercially

Google has used page speed as a ranking signal for years. You are competing against companies whose sites load in well under a second. Even with better content than theirs, you start every search from behind.

How I measured this. Loaded your homepage in a real browser and recorded what it actually downloaded and when, then repeated the test on a competitor's site. These are observed figures, not estimates from a scoring tool.

The business impact

What this is costing you right now.

None of the following is hypothetical. Each one is happening on your live site today.

Every visitor who arrives from Google sees a blank screen for seven seconds. Most of them leave before your page appears. You pay for that traffic in ranking position, and you get nothing back from it.

Your credibility is undermined before anyone reads a word. The homepage claims "0% Reduction in Vehicle Theft". Your contact page title reads "Wireless Wireless Vehicle Solutions". There is a blank white image sitting on your homepage. Fleet managers buying compliance equipment notice this kind of thing.

Your newest article gives customers out-of-date compliance information. It announces a DVS deadline "extended till May 2025". On a subject where getting it wrong means fines, that does real damage to trust.

You're invisible for the searches that matter most. DVS and Progressive Safe System demand is at its peak, and you have one good page on it against competitors running full guides. Meanwhile 45 of your 126 pages are empty tag archives adding nothing.

No buyer can find a single review of you. I searched. There is no Trustpilot presence and no findable Google reviews. Your competitors publish named case studies with fleet sizes and results. In a considered B2B purchase, that gap decides deals.

Your products can't appear properly in search results. 29 products with none of the data Google needs to display them with prices. Competitors selling the same DVS kits can appear with price and availability; you can't.

The honest summary

You have a decent business with a website that actively works against it. Nothing here requires a rethink of what Neatcom does or how it sells — it requires someone to take the site seriously for a quarter.

What I haven't seen yet. I ran this review from the outside. I have not had access to your Google Search Console, your analytics, or the WordPress admin, so this contains no data on what you currently rank for or where your traffic comes from. Connecting those is the first thing I'd do — it turns every recommendation here from a well-founded judgement into a measured one.

The plan

What I'd do, and in what order.

Sequenced so the things that cost least and return fastest happen first.

Phase 1 — Stop the bleeding

Weeks 1–2
  • Fix the speed. Turn on image compression, replace the autoplay videos with still images that play on click, and bundle the 92 separate files. Target: under 2 MB, loading in under 2.5 seconds.
  • Fix the embarrassing things. The "0%" statistics, the two typos, the blank image, the broken product headings.
  • Clean up what Google sees. Remove the three conflicting sitemaps, stop the 45 empty pages being indexed, fix the mobile zoom fault, and repair the tacho link that currently redirects to an image file.
  • Connect the measurement. Search Console and analytics, so everything after this is tracked rather than assumed.

Phase 2 — Make the site sell

Weeks 3–8
  • Own the DVS and Progressive Safe System topic. Your strongest page becomes the hub of a proper guide — star ratings, what PSS requires, evidence needed, what it costs. This is the highest-intent search demand in your market.
  • Rebuild the tachograph page. It should be the strongest page on the site rather than one of the thinnest — it currently never mentions remote download, calibration, or the 28- and 90-day download deadlines buyers search for.
  • Make the 29 products visible to Google with proper product data, real descriptions, and specifications written as text rather than baked into images.
  • Refresh the out-of-date articles and verify every compliance claim against TfL directly.
  • Start collecting reviews and write three named case studies. This is the single biggest trust gap against your competitors.

Phase 3 — Build the pipeline

Ongoing
  • Publish consistently — two useful articles a month, written for fleet managers facing a compliance decision, not for search engines.
  • Win locally. Coventry, Birmingham and the wider Midlands are winnable; national head terms are not, yet.
  • Report monthly against the baseline on the next page, so you can see exactly what the work bought.

The website

On rebuilding the site.

You've mentioned you want the website redone. Having looked at it closely, I agree — and I can tell you precisely why, which matters more than the opinion.

Why patching isn't enough

The site is built in a page builder with heavy add-ons, layered on a theme that fights it. That's why the homepage needs 46 stylesheets and 46 scripts to show what is essentially text and pictures.

I can get the speed down substantially by configuring what's there — but the ceiling is set by the architecture. A clean rebuild removes the ceiling instead of raising it.

There's also a structural problem no amount of tuning fixes: the site has 126 pages, 45 of which are empty archives, while the pages that should be selling — services, products — are thin.

What I'd keep

A rebuild doesn't mean throwing away what works.

  • Your DVS page content — it's the best thing on the site.
  • Your URLs, so you keep whatever standing you've built.
  • Your brand and product photography, properly compressed.
  • The FAQ content, marked up so it can appear directly in Google.

What changes

  • Built for speed first — a target of under 1 second, which your competitors already manage.
  • Structured around how fleet buyers actually search: by regulation, by vehicle type, by problem.
  • Every page with a clear next step — quote, demo, or call.
  • Editable by your team without needing a developer for a text change.

A sensible sequence

Phase 1 fixes are worth doing on the current site even with a rebuild planned — they're quick, they recover ground immediately, and the content work carries straight across. I'd rather you saw results in week two than waited three months for a launch.
